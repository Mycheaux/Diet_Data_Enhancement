# Nitrate/NO-Axis Diet And Oral Microbiome: Brief Result Notes

## Main Observed Patterns

- **Vegetable nitrate**
  - `Neisseria` increases.
  - `Burkholderiaceae` increases.
  - `Prevotella` / `Prevotellaceae` decreases.
  - `Moraxellaceae` decreases.
  - `Megasphaera` decreases.
  - Shannon diversity appears lower with higher vegetable nitrate.

- **Nitrite / processed nitrite**
  - `Prevotella` increases.
  - Shannon diversity increases.
  - `Rothia dentocariosa` decreases for processed nitrite.

- **Nitroso axis**
  - `Rothia dentocariosa` increases.
  - `Veillonella` decreases.
  - `Prevotellaceae` decreases.

- **Arginine / nitric oxide axis**
  - `Neisseriaceae` / `GGB6675` increases.
  - `Prevotella` decreases.

## Literature Fit

- The **vegetable nitrate pattern makes strong biological sense**.
  - Nitrate studies repeatedly report increased `Neisseria` and `Rothia`.
  - Nitrate studies often report decreased `Prevotella` and `Veillonella`.
  - This supports a nitrate-associated shift toward a more nitrate-cycle-compatible oral community.

- Lower Shannon diversity with vegetable nitrate is plausible.
  - It may reflect ecological specialization rather than worse oral health.
  - Nitrate may selectively favor nitrate/NO-cycle taxa while suppressing anaerobic/dysbiosis-associated taxa.

- `Prevotella` decreasing with vegetable nitrate is literature-compatible.
  - Some `Prevotella` can reduce nitrate, but intervention studies often show Prevotella depletion after nitrate exposure.

- `Veillonella` decreasing with nitrate/nitroso is also literature-compatible.
  - `Veillonella` can reduce nitrate, but often decreases after nitrate supplementation, possibly due to nitrate-driven pH/ecology changes.

- Processed nitrite behaving differently from vegetable nitrate is interesting.
  - Processed nitrite showing `Prevotella` and Shannon increase may suggest a different diet/ecology axis than vegetable nitrate.
  - This separation is important because it argues against a generic “all nitrate/nitrite foods do the same thing” result.

## Why This Is Interesting

- The result may separate **vegetable nitrate** from **processed nitrite/nitroso food patterns**.
- Vegetable nitrate appears linked to a more selective oral ecology:
  - `Neisseria` up
  - `Prevotella` / `Prevotellaceae` down
  - `Veillonella`/anaerobic features down
  - Shannon down
- Processed nitrite appears to move differently:
  - `Prevotella` up
  - Shannon up
  - `Rothia dentocariosa` down
- This contrast could be a strong paper angle:
  - Same broad nitrogen chemistry, different dietary sources, different oral microbial ecology.

## Possible Paper Claim

- Habitual vegetable nitrate intake is associated with an oral microbiome state resembling nitrate-supplementation studies: increased `Neisseria` and reduced `Prevotella`/`Veillonella`-like anaerobic taxa.
- Processed nitrite/nitroso-related food axes show distinct or opposite oral microbial patterns.
- This suggests the **food matrix and dietary context** may determine whether nitrogen-related exposures align with a potentially beneficial nitrate-cycle oral ecology.

## Most Important Follow-Up Tests

- Confirm if `Rothia` and `Neisseria` increase for vegetable nitrate after adjustment.
- Confirm if `Prevotella`, `Prevotellaceae`, `Veillonella`, and `Megasphaera` decrease for vegetable nitrate after adjustment.
- Test a balance score:
  - `Neisseria + Rothia`
  - minus
  - `Prevotella + Veillonella + Megasphaera`
- Compare this balance across:
  - vegetable nitrate
  - processed nitrite
  - nitroso axis
  - arginine/NO axis
- Check whether this balance associates with:
  - blood pressure
  - cholesterol/lipids
  - HbA1c/glucose
  - inflammation markers
  - oral/dental health proxies if available

## Cautions

- `GGB` taxa are less interpretable than named genera/species.
- `Burkholderiaceae` and `Moraxellaceae` need careful follow-up before highlighting.
- Processed nitrite may capture broader processed-food patterns, not nitrite chemistry alone.
- The strongest paper story should focus on reproducible taxa/pathways, not single weak taxa.

## Best Current Story

- **Vegetable nitrate:** lower-diversity, more `Neisseria`-shifted, less `Prevotella`/anaerobe-heavy oral ecology.
- **Processed nitrite:** higher-diversity, more `Prevotella`-associated oral ecology, possibly reflecting a different dietary context.
- **Nitroso/arginine axes:** partially overlap with vegetable nitrate but may reveal distinct nitric-oxide-related ecology.
